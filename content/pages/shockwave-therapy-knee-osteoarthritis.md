## Find Relief From Knee Arthritis Without Surgery

Knee osteoarthritis can make simple things feel hard. Walking across the parking lot, climbing a few stairs, or standing to cook dinner can leave your knees aching and stiff. Many adults in Amarillo feel this even more when the weather cools down and joints do not like that extra chill in the air.

If you feel stuck between living with pain and jumping into surgery, there is another option. Extracorporeal shockwave therapy, often called shockwave or SoftWave-type therapy, is a non-surgical, drug-free treatment for stubborn knee pain. In this article, we will share how it works, who it tends to help the most, who should avoid it, what a visit feels like, and what kinds of results people commonly notice over time.

## How Shockwave Therapy Eases Knee Osteoarthritis

Shockwave therapy uses sound waves that travel through the skin into the area around your knee joint. These are not electric shocks. They are pulses of mechanical energy that gently stress the tissues and signal the body that repair work is needed.

Those sound waves can help by:

- Increasing blood flow to the area
- Waking up slow or “asleep” cells inside your tissues
- Supporting the body’s natural healing response over time
- Breaking up some tight, irritated points in muscles and soft tissue

For knee osteoarthritis, that can mean:

- Less pain and soreness with daily activity
- Lower irritation in the joint and surrounding tissues
- Easier bending and straightening of the knee
- Better support for the cartilage and ligaments around the joint

Some people use shockwave therapy to try to delay or even avoid joint replacement. It does not rebuild a brand new knee, but it may help calm pain and improve function enough that walking, work, and hobbies feel more doable again.

- Corrective exercises to build strength and stability

## Who Gets the Best Results From Shockwave Knee Care

Not everyone with knee osteoarthritis is in the same place. Some still walk and work every day but feel nagging pain. Others are closer to needing surgery. Shockwave therapy tends to give the best results for certain groups.

Ideal candidates often include adults who:

- Have mild to moderate knee osteoarthritis
- Feel recurring stiffness after sitting or with more activity
- Notice aching that gets worse in colder or damp weather
- Have pain that limits walking, standing, work, or recreation
- Want to avoid surgery or heavy use of pain medications if possible

In Amarillo, many people like to stay active in the fall. Walking at the park, ranch work, hunting, school sports events, and holiday travel all ask a lot from your knees. When your joints are already irritated, these activities can feel harder than they should.

People often find strong value in shockwave care when their goals are realistic. Typical goals sound like:

- Less daily pain, especially at the end of the day
- Easier time with stairs, standing from a chair, or getting out of a vehicle
- Better sleep because the knee is not throbbing at night
- More confidence to stay active, instead of “saving” the knees by resting all the time

Shockwave therapy is not meant to make you a pro athlete. It is designed to help you do normal life with less pain so you can enjoy your day without thinking about your knees every step.

## When Shockwave Therapy Is Not the Right Choice

Like any medical treatment, shockwave therapy is not right for everyone. There are times when it may be unsafe or unlikely to give good results.

People usually should not have shockwave therapy over the knee if they have:

- An active infection in or around the joint
- A known blood clotting disorder in that area
- Pregnancy over the treatment area
- Open wounds, sores, or severe skin problems at the site
- A recent fracture or major trauma around the knee
- Certain types of tumors or active cancer at or near the knee

Shockwave can also be less helpful for those with very advanced knee arthritis. If the joint is severely bone-on-bone, has large deformity, or has already been through multiple failed procedures, a different plan might be better. This is where a careful exam matters.

At One Light Medical, we look at:

- Your medical history and current diagnoses
- Medications you take, including blood thinners and bone-related drugs
- Imaging such as X-rays or other studies, if available
- Your pain pattern, what makes it better or worse
- How you walk, bend, squat, and move through your day

This helps us decide if shockwave therapy makes sense, or if another path is safer and more likely to help.

## What to Expect During Treatment and Recovery

If you are new to shockwave therapy, not knowing what it feels like can be a little stressful. The good news is most people handle it well, and sessions are usually short.

A typical visit for knee shockwave at our clinic may include:

1. A consultation to talk about your symptoms and goals.
2. An orthopedic and movement exam to see how your knee is working.
3. Positioning you comfortably so the knee is easy to reach.
4. Applying a small amount of ultrasound gel on the skin so the sound waves travel well.
5. Using the shockwave handpiece to deliver pulses around key areas of the knee.

People often describe the feeling as tapping, pressure, or quick pulses. Some spots may feel more tender, especially where tissues are irritated. We can adjust the settings in real time to help keep you as comfortable as possible while still giving a useful dose.

After treatment, you might notice:

- Mild soreness, like you did a workout
- Temporary warmth or redness in the area
- A sense of lightness or looser movement in the joint

Results usually build over a series of sessions, not overnight. Many plans include several treatments spread over a number of weeks. People often start noticing changes such as less pain with walking or easier bending partway through the plan, with further improvement after the full series.

How long results last can depend on what you do outside the clinic. When shockwave is paired with:

- Simple home exercises for strength and mobility
- Joint-friendly habits, such as smart activity pacing
- Support for healthy weight and overall movement

benefits often hold longer, and your knees have a better chance of staying happier during daily life.
