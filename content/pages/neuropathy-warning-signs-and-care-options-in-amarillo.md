Peripheral neuropathy happens when nerves outside the brain and spinal cord stop working the way they should. These are the nerves that help you feel hot and cold, move your muscles and know where your feet are on the floor. When they are irritated or damaged, they can send mixed signals or no signals at all.

In a community like Amarillo, neuropathy is common for many reasons. People may live with diabetes, past injuries, repetitive work, or side effects from chemotherapy. Aging, long days on concrete floors, or years of standing and lifting can also play a part.

Because the early signs can be mild, people often brush them off. A little tingling in the toes, some burning at night, or a “pins and needles” feeling after sitting too long. It is easy to blame it on shoes, age, or the weather. But when those feelings come more often or last longer, it is a sign your nerves are asking for help.

Catching these changes early and getting [neuropathy treatment in Amarillo](/neuropathy/) can protect your long-term mobility and independence. When cooler, windy days move in and joints feel stiffer, nerve pain and balance issues can feel worse. Paying attention now can make walking, working, and sleeping more comfortable later on.

## Early Warning Signs You Should Never Ignore

Neuropathy rarely shows up out of nowhere. It usually starts with small changes that build over time. These early clues are the best time to get checked before more serious damage sets in.

Common sensory changes include:

- Persistent numbness in toes, feet, or fingers
- Tingling or “pins and needles” that do not fully go away
- Burning or “electric shock” sensations
- Extra sensitivity, where even light touch or bedsheets feel painful
- Feeling like your socks are bunched up or shoes are wrinkled when they are flat

You might also notice functional changes, such as:

- Losing your balance more easily, especially in the dark
- Dropping items or feeling clumsy with your hands
- Tripping on flat surfaces or catching your toes on steps
- Trouble feeling the gas or brake pedals while driving
- Pain, burning, or tingling that gets worse at night and interrupts sleep

Sometimes the skin and nails give early hints too. These appearance changes can include:

- Shiny or thinning skin on the feet
- Changes in nail growth or hair growth on the lower legs and feet
- One foot feeling warmer or cooler than the other
- Cuts, blisters, or sores on the feet that seem slow to heal

These warning signs do not always mean permanent nerve damage, but they should never be ignored. This stage is often the ideal time to consider [neuropathy treatment in Amarillo](/neuropathy/), when nerves may respond better to care and daily life is not yet completely disrupted.

## Red Flag Symptoms That Need Prompt Care

Some symptoms point to more serious nerve or spine problems and should be taken more seriously. These are signs that you should not wait and see what happens.

Watch for sudden changes like:

- Rapid onset of weakness in the legs, feet, arms, or hands
- Severe burning pain that comes on quickly
- Numbness or tingling that spreads fast up the legs or arms

Loss of function is another big red flag. This can look like:

- Struggling to walk across a room without holding on to furniture
- Trouble climbing stairs that used to be easy
- Difficulty standing up from a chair without using your arms
- Hands that cannot manage small tasks, like buttoning a shirt or holding utensils

Neuropathy can be more concerning when it happens along with other health issues. For example:

- Ongoing nerve symptoms with poorly controlled blood sugar
- New or worsening neuropathy after chemotherapy
- Neuropathy in the setting of an autoimmune condition
- Symptoms that start soon after a major infection

If you suddenly cannot walk well, cannot move part of your body, lose bladder or bowel control, or have very strong, sharp back pain with fast-spreading numbness, urgent or emergency care is usually the safest choice. For symptoms that are new but not severe, a same-week neuropathy evaluation with a provider in Amarillo is often appropriate.

## How Neuropathy Is Evaluated at an Integrative Clinic

At an integrative clinic, the first step is a detailed history and exam. We listen to your story, including:

- When symptoms started and how they have changed
- Medical conditions like diabetes, past injuries, and surgeries
- Medications, chemo history, and family history
- Work demands, daily activity, and hobbies that might stress the nerves

Next, we perform neurologic and orthopedic testing to see which nerves and tissues are involved. In-office assessments may include:

- Watching how you stand, walk, turn, and change directions
- Testing strength in the feet, legs, hands, and arms
- Checking reflexes with a reflex hammer
- Sensation tests such as vibration, light touch, and temperature
- Simple balance tests with feet together, in a line, or on one leg

In some cases, imaging or other diagnostic studies may be recommended to look at the spine, joints, or other areas. The goal is not only to label your symptoms as neuropathy but to understand why your nerves are stressed.

An integrative approach looks beyond “nerve pills” alone. It takes into account spinal changes, joint mechanics, posture, inflammation, and metabolic factors. ## Non-Surgical Options to Calm Nerves and Protect Mobility

For many people, nerve symptoms can be managed with non-surgical care that aims to ease irritation and improve how the nerves function over time.

[SoftWave TRT](/softwave-trt-treatment/) and other nerve-focused therapies use non-invasive acoustic waves over the area of pain or numbness. These treatments are designed to:

- Support local blood flow around irritated nerves
- Help calm inflammation in soft tissues
- Encourage the body’s natural healing response over time

When neuropathy is linked to disc or spine issues, [spinal decompression](/spinal-decompression/) and [chiropractic care](/chiropractic-care/) may play a role. Gentle decompression can help relieve pressure on spinal nerves, while chiropractic adjustments are used to improve joint alignment and motion. As pressure on irritated nerves decreases, radiating pain, burning, or weakness in the arms or legs may ease.

Lifestyle and weight management support are also key pieces. Extra body weight can add stress on joints and nerves and make movement harder. Guided weight loss, safe movement plans, and support for better metabolic health may help:

- Reduce pressure on feet, ankles, knees, and hips
- Improve blood flow to the lower legs and feet
- Make it easier to stay active even when the weather turns cooler
- Support better balance and confidence with walking

By combining these tools, care can be tailored to your needs rather than relying on a single method.
